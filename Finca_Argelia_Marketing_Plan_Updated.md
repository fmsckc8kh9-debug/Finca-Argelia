PHASE 3: FOB PRICING --- PUERTO QUETZAL

3.1 How Specialty Coffee Pricing Works

There are two pricing models in the specialty market. Understanding both
is essential before you walk into any negotiation.

Model A: Differential Pricing (C-Market + Premium)

This is the most common model in the green coffee trade. Your price is
quoted as the ICE Arabica futures price (the "C-market") plus a fixed
differential. For example, if the C-market is \$2.90/lb and your
differential is +\$0.50, your FOB price is \$3.40/lb.

-   **How it moves:** When the C-market goes up, your FOB price goes up
    by the same amount. When it drops, your price drops. Your premium
    (the differential) stays fixed.

-   **Advantage:** You capture upside when the market rises. Buyers are
    accustomed to this model.

-   **Risk:** If the C-market crashes, your price drops with it, even if
    your production costs haven't changed.

Model B: Fixed Price

You and the buyer agree on a flat \$/lb FOB price regardless of what the
C-market does. For example, \$3.50/lb FOB Puerto Quetzal, locked in for
the contract.

-   **How it moves:** It doesn't. That's the point. Both sides have
    price certainty.

-   **Advantage:** You can budget and plan with a known income. Many
    roasters prefer this because they need to set retail prices.

-   **Risk:** If the C-market surges (as it did in 2025, hitting over
    \$4.00/lb), you're locked into a lower price.

+-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **Recommendation for Finca Argelia**                                  |
|                                                                       |
| Use differential pricing for spot sales and first-time buyers. Use    |
| fixed pricing only for multi-year contracts where the buyer commits   |
| to guaranteed volume---and set the fixed price with a floor that      |
| covers your production costs plus a healthy margin. In the current    |
| volatile market, fixed-price deals should be revisited annually.      |

3.4 Understanding Your Cost Base

Before you negotiate, you must know your breakeven. Calculate your total
cost per pound to produce and deliver coffee FOB Puerto Quetzal.
Include: farm labor (picking, pruning, fertilization), wet mill costs,
drying, transport to port, dry mill/export prep, port handling, and
export documentation. Add your desired margin on top. Never sell below
your full cost---even if a buyer pressures you.

3.5 Pricing Tactics at the Show

-   **Lead with value, not price.** Talk about your family, the Ossaye
    history, your process, your conservation work. Let the buyer ask
    about price---don't volunteer it first.

-   **Quote in writing.** When asked, say: "I'll send you a formal offer
    with pricing within 48 hours." This avoids show-floor negotiation
    and gives you time to think.

-   **Know your walk-away number.** Decide before the show: what is the
    absolute minimum you will accept per pound? Anything below that, you
    politely decline.

-   **Offer volume incentives.** If a buyer commits to 10,000+ lbs, you
    can offer a slightly better differential. Structure it: 5,000 lbs at
    C+\$0.50, 10,000+ lbs at C+\$0.40.

-   **Multi-year commitments deserve better terms.** A buyer who commits
    to purchasing from you for 2--3 years reduces your sales risk.
    Reward that with a \$0.05--\$0.10 better differential.

4.2 The Altitude Conversation --- How to Handle It

At 1,067 masl (3,500 ft), you're below what most buyers consider
"high-grown" for Guatemala (typically 1,200+ masl for SHB, Strictly Hard
Bean, classification). Buyers will notice this. Here's how to handle it
honestly and confidently:

-   **Don't hide it.** It's on every spec sheet and buyers know
    Guatemala's geography. Trying to avoid the question makes you look
    evasive.

-   **Frame it as your terroir.** "Our boca costa position on
    Tecuamburro's slopes---where the humid Pacific lowlands meet the
    cooler highlands, with deep volcanic clay soil and steady
    rainfall---produces a different profile than Huehuetenango or
    Antigua. We get a heavier body, more chocolate and caramel
    sweetness, and a smoother acidity. For roasters looking for a
    versatile, crowd-pleasing single origin or an espresso-blend
    component, that's exactly what they want."

-   **Let the cup score speak.** "We're at 1,067 meters and we're
    scoring 83 SCA. That tells you our agronomy and processing are
    dialed in---our family has had 135 years to figure out what works on
    this mountain, with 40-year-old Bourbon trees rooted deep in
    volcanic clay and fed by three natural springs."

-   **Target the right buyers.** Some roasters specifically want the
    lower-acid, fuller-body profile for espresso blends, drip coffee
    programs, and subscription boxes. That's your ideal customer---not
    the roaster chasing the highest-altitude Geisha.

    forest. Let them experience the place and the family. This deepens
    the relationship enormously.

-   **Ongoing:** Keep every contact in a simple CRM or spreadsheet.
    Track: name, company, interest level, sample sent (Y/N), follow-up
    dates, outcome.
